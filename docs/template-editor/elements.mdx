# Elements

:::tip[In short]
Groups arrange the page; fields, lists, and trackers hold values. A group is a section or a card, a list holds entries or table rows: its **Kind** switches between them. Groups split into up to four columns and can link to a documentation page.
:::

## Kinds of groups and lists \{#kinds}

A **Group** holds other elements, as a **Section** or a **Card**. A **List** holds what readers add one by one, as **Entries** or a **Table**. The **Kind** at the top of **Content** switches between them; the outline and the page name both, such as "Group · Section" or "List · Table".

- **Section ↔ Card** keeps the title, the contents, the columns, and the documentation link. A card made from a section can still be folded.
- **Entries → Table** makes the entry field the first column. **Table → Entries** makes the first column the entry field; other column types become text, and the other columns are removed after you confirm.
- What one kind has and the other lacks (a card's hidden title, preset entries, row limits) comes back when you switch back before closing the editor.
- **Table** is not offered for lists the game keeps and for lists that suggest catalog entries.
- Entries and table rows are stored differently. When documents already hold values of the earlier kind, **Save** names the list and asks first; the values stay stored and show again if you switch back.

## Sections \{#sections}

A section is a large block with a heading and a colored bar. Readers fold and unfold it; **Starts collapsed** keeps it folded until the reader opens it once (the choice is then remembered). Neighbouring sections alternate the bar's color by themselves.

## Cards \{#groups}

A card is a titled box inside a page or section. **Show title** hides the heading while keeping the card; **Collapsible** lets readers fold it (only with a visible title).

## Documentation link \{#documentation-link}

Sections and cards take a **Documentation link**: a **?** next to the heading opens that page in a new tab. Two forms are accepted:

- **A page of this site's documentation**: the address from the browser after the domain, starting with `/docs/`, for example `/docs/wod-v5/rules/dice-pools#reading-the-dice`. The part after `#` jumps to a heading: open the page, hover a heading, and copy its link. Readers get the page in their own language — write the English address and Russian readers land on the Russian page.
- **An external page**: a full address starting with `https://`, for example a wiki article.

Anything else — an address without `/docs/`, `http://`, or another scheme — is listed as an issue and is not shown on the page.

## Columns \{#columns}

Pages, sections, and cards lay out their elements in 1 to 4 **Columns**. With **Equal column widths** off, each column gets a width: widths are proportional, so 2 and 1 make the first column twice as wide. Without pinned elements, elements fill the columns in order. **Column in parent** pins an element to one column; once any element of a container is pinned, the elements left on **Auto** stack in the first column. **Spans columns** stretches an element over 2 to 4 columns — a wide field beside a narrow one, or a full-width row; spans work while no element of the container is pinned. On a phone every container shows one column. An empty column shows a drop zone in the editor.

## Fields \{#fields}

A field holds one value. Its **Label** is shown next to it (**Show label** hides it), **Help text** explains it on hover, and **Required** marks it for the reader without blocking anything. **Label position** puts the label **Above the value** in small grey type, or **Beside the value** in the larger type of the sheet's attribute rows; ratings and derived values start beside, other fields above. An image always keeps its label above: the picture and its upload controls need the whole width.

| Type                   | What the reader gets                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Text**               | A line of text, or a larger box with **Multiline**; a **Placeholder** shows while it is empty.                                                                                                           |
| **Number**             | A number kept within **Min**, **Max**, and **Step**; arrow keys step it.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Toggle**             | A dot: transparent when off, filled when on.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Choice**             | One option from a list, or several with **Allow multiple values**; **Hide unselected options** shows only the chosen ones until the reader opens the list. A choice can take its options from a catalog. |
| **Rating**             | A row like the sheet's attributes: dots or a number from **Min** to **Max**; see [Ratings](#rating).                                                                                                     |
| **Resource**           | A current value and a maximum, such as a pool of points. **Display: Tracker** draws it as one row of boxes, as for [built-in pools](#built-in-parts), up to 20 boxes.                                    |
| **Derived value**      | A number computed by a [formula](./values.mdx#formulas); readers cannot type into it.                                                                                                                    |
| **Image**              | A picture from the device or an `https://` address.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Document reference** | Links to other documents of the allowed types; readers open them from the field; see [Document references](#references).                                                                                 |
| **Tracker**            | Boxes the reader marks by level, for health, stress, or any other burden; see [Trackers](#trackers).                                                                                                     |

## Ratings \{#rating}

A rating reads like an attribute row on the shipped sheets: the label on the left, the dots on the right. **Presentation** chooses **Dots** (one dot per point) or **Number** (a number box). Templates saved with the older "boxes" presentation open as dots and keep their values.

- **Min** is a floor, not a dot: the dots up to it stay filled in a darker shade, and the reader cannot go below it.
- **Max** sets the number of dots. A [maximum from a value or formula](./values.mdx#limits-from-values) replaces it while it can be computed, up to 100 dots; when it drops below the stored value, the value is kept and shown in parentheses until the maximum rises again.
- **Text input (for example a specialization)** adds a line between the label and the dots; on narrow screens it moves under the label.
- **Show current / maximum** writes "3 / 5" after the dots; with **Number** it adds "/ 5" inside the number box, like a resource whose maximum cannot be edited.
- **Die symbol (roll the rating)** rolls the value the way the document's game rolls an attribute of the same value; the roll names the rating.
- **Flags** (dots only): **S** (specialization), **P** (practiced), and **E** (experienced) add the same switches as a trait row. They change the roll only where the game's dice rule uses them: the Star Wars sheet does, V5 does not.

Formulas and display conditions read the rating's number; its text and flags do not change it. Ratings with the same [shared value key](./values.mdx#shared-value-key) share the number, the text, and the flags.

## Document references \{#references}

**Allowed document kinds** lists the kinds of the page's own setting, each once: the setting's own kinds (a Hunter page offers the hunter), the ruleset's shared kinds (the V5 mortal), and the kinds you created in that setting or for the whole ruleset. Kinds of other games and of your other settings are not offered, even when they have the same name. When two offered kinds would read the same, the setting is added to the name; your own kind of the same name in the same setting is marked "(yours)". A new reference points at the page's own kind.

On the sheet, the search offers the reader's documents of those kinds from the same setting, never the document itself.

- A reference saved earlier may point at a document of another setting. It stays: the entry shows the document's title, notes "outside this setting", opens it, and can be removed. Once removed, that document is not offered again.
- A document that was deleted shows "Linked document no longer exists" until the reader removes it.
- A kind the setting no longer has (a deleted type, or one from another setting) stays checked and is marked "(unavailable)", and the template's problems list names it. Uncheck it to drop it; the last kind cannot be unchecked.

## Tables \{#tables}

A table is a list of rows with the same columns; every column is a field. **Min rows** and **Max rows** bound how many rows readers can add. Reorder the columns with their grip or their up and down arrows: filled values stay with their column.

On the sheet, readers reorder rows and entries of tables and lists: drag a row by its grip, use its up and down arrows, or press `Alt+↑` / `Alt+↓` in the row. The new order is saved with the document. Read-only documents show no move controls.

## Lists \{#lists}

A list of kind **Entries** holds entries added one by one: your own entries, or a list the game already keeps for the document (skills, merits and flaws, equipment).

A list with its own entries sets up one **Entry**: pick its type — text, number, toggle, choice, rating, resource, document reference, or image — and its settings, as for a field of that type. Every entry the reader adds is a copy of it. A new list starts with the classic trait row: a rating with dots, the S/P/E flags, and a die.

- **Entries are named**: each entry gets a name the reader types, such as "Firearms" next to its dots. Turn it off for lists of notes or pictures: then each entry shows the entry's label, or nothing when **Show label** is off.
- **Preset entries** start a new document with ready rows: their names, and their numbers where the entry holds one. Presets need named entries.
- Each entry has its own remove button: at the end of the row, or above a block such as a picture or several lines of text.

Changing the entry type or turning names off can hide values readers already stored. **Save** then lists how many values or names each list would stop showing and asks first. The values stay stored: changing the entry back shows them again, except for entries edited in between.

## Trackers \{#trackers}

A tracker is a set of levels (such as Bruised … Incapacitated) with boxes the reader marks. A click moves a box to the next fill mark, and from the last one back to empty. A new tracker starts as a seven-level health track with its penalties and two marks.

- **Source**: **Own values** keeps the marks with the page. A game's built-in track (health, damage, willpower, members) keeps them in the document itself, so every page of that document shows the same marks; see [Built-in trackers](#built-in-trackers).
- **Display**: **Table** (a row per level), **Strip** (a row of boxes per column), or **One line** (small boxes with the label inline, for brief pages; no length buttons and no legend).
- **Marks**: up to five. Each has a name, a symbol of one or two characters, and a fill: amber, red, violet, green, ink, or accent (the rating dots' color) follow the light and dark themes, or pick your own color. **Start from…** offers one mark, two (bashing ╱, lethal ×), or three as in WoD 20th (adding aggravated ✱ in violet), or **Points: current and maximum**. The order is the click order, and a later mark counts as heavier.
- **Layer** of a mark: a **Fill** colors the box behind its symbol; an **Outline** draws a ring in the mark's color around the box. A box holds one fill and one outline at once, so an outline can show a maximum (Force Points) or a condition (bleeding) next to the damage. A click changes the fill. The **outline action** puts the first outline mark on by the same rules: a right click, **Shift+Enter** or **Shift+Space** on a box, or a long press on a phone. Other outline marks come from the legend. A tracker whose marks are all outlines cycles them with a plain click. Without outline marks, the right click opens the browser's menu as usual.
- **Levels**: up to twenty, each with a name and a short value, such as a penalty "-1", a bonus "+2", or a word. The value column has its own title and can be hidden. Move levels and marks with the up and down arrows: stored marks stay on their level and kind.
- **Columns**: **Marks** (boxes) or **Text** (a short note per level, such as "Source"). A column covers all levels or the first few shown. With **Readers add copies (A, B, C…)** the reader adds copies of the column, up to the number you set, like the members of a fodder group; removing a copy that holds marks asks first. A strip shows marks columns only.
- **Readers switch the length**: list the lengths and tick the levels each shows, like a fodder group's 3, 5, and 7. When the reader shortens the tracker over marked levels, it asks first; then each mark keeps its place in the shown order and the marks past the new end fold into its last level, the heaviest one winning.
- **Marks fill from the start**: marking a box marks every box before it, like a rating's dots; marking the last marked box takes just that one off. Each layer runs on its own, so the outline can frame a maximum and the fill count the points inside it, and the legend's marks run the same way. **Fills stay inside the outline** stops a fill at the last framed box.
- **Reading the marks**: the **Total row** shows, for each marks copy, either the value of the deepest marked level (**Deepest level**) or a **Count** of the filled boxes, with the framed ones after a slash ("2 / 5") when the tracker has outline marks, under a thicker divider. **Mark a copy as out** strikes a copy through once its last shown level is marked. The total and "out" read the fills; outlines never count. **Name the marks under the tracker** adds a legend of the marks' symbols and names; it is off by default, built-in trackers included.
- **Marking with the legend**: with the legend on, the reader presses a mark in it (a yellow frame shows it is picked); each click then puts that mark on a box in one step, or takes it off when the box already has it. Pressing it again or Escape stops. Shortening folds fills and outlines each on their own.

Removing levels, marks, columns, or copies can hide marks and notes readers already stored. **Save** then says how many each tracker would stop showing and asks first. The values stay stored, but a removed part added again is a new part, so they do not show again.

A tracker cannot be a table column or a list entry: its copies already repeat it.

### Built-in trackers \{#built-in-trackers}

A game's own track takes the same settings: the display, the names and values of its levels (a level you leave empty keeps the game's name and penalty), the names, symbols, and fills of its marks, extra columns, the total row, and the legend, which works as a brush here too. Its marks are always fills: the game keeps one mark per box. The game keeps the number and order of levels, a length computed from traits (V5 health and willpower), the number of marks, and the members of a group; lengths and "out" follow the game's rules. Columns you add keep their values with the page, next to the document's own marks.

Pages saved before this setting existed keep their look: **Compact** reads as **One line**, and a stored track view keeps its display.

## Built-in page parts \{#built-in-parts}

Shipped pages also use built-in parts: traits, resources, and lists the game itself keeps for the document. They look like the fields above and store their values in the document, so every page of that document shows the same numbers.

A pool resource (a current value and a maximum, such as Force Points or Willpower) shows as dots by default. **Display: Tracker** draws it as one row of boxes instead: the maximum framed, the current points filled, and the count after them ("2 / 5"). **Row** sits with rating rows (label on the left, boxes the size of rating dots in the accent color); **Strip** and **One line** look like the tracker displays. You can rename, re-symbol, and recolor the two marks and turn on the legend; which mark fills and which frames is fixed. A click sets the current points, the outline action sets the maximum, and both keep the game's limits: **Current at least** and **Maximum at least** take a value or a formula, and the boxes they hold are drawn darker and stay. A pool whose current value raises its maximum (Star Wars Willpower) raises it from the tracker too.
