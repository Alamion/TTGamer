# Skills and Specialties

_Step 5 of 9_

:::tip[In short]
Choose one spread — **Jack of all trades**, **Balanced**, or **Specialist** — place the dots, then add one free **Specialty** (plus automatic ones for Academics, Craft, Performance, and Science).
:::

## Why it matters at the table

Skills are what your hunter learned. A roll adds a Skill to an Attribute, so a hunter with no dots in Firearms is not helpless with a gun — just unreliable.

## Decide

- Does your hunter know a little about everything, a solid amount about several things, or a lot about a few?
- Which Skills does their day job give them?
- Which Skills did they pick up since the Reckoning?

| Spread             | At 4 | At 3 | At 2 | At 1 |
| ------------------ | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Jack of all trades | —    | 1    | 8    | 10   |
| Balanced           | —    | 3    | 5    | 7    |
| Specialist         | 1    | 3    | 3    | 3    |

Then:

- Add **one Specialty** of your choice to a Skill you have dots in.
- If you have dots in **Academics, Craft, Performance,** or **Science**, each of them gets a Specialty too — those Skills are too broad without one.

## On your sheet

Set dots, then type the Specialty in the line next to its Skill. Separate several with commas: _Trauma, Triage_.

:::note[Example: Lena]
Lena takes **Balanced**. At 3: **Medicine, Driving, Awareness**. At 2: **Athletics, Insight, Persuasion, Streetwise, Occult**. At 1: **Brawl, Firearms, Stealth, Etiquette, Investigation, Science, Technology**. Her free Specialty is _Medicine (Trauma)_; Science brings _Science (Pharmacology)_ with it.
:::

## Common questions

<details>
<summary>Where does Occult come from for a paramedic?</summary>

From the Reckoning. It is fine — good, even — for a few Skills to come from the hunt rather than the job.

</details>

:::info[Checkpoint]

- The dot counts match your spread.
- At least one Specialty, plus one for each of Academics, Craft, Performance, Science you took.
  :::
