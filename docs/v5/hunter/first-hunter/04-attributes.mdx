# Attributes

_Step 4 of 9_

:::tip[In short]
Put **one** Attribute at 4, **three** at 3, **four** at 2, and **one** at 1.
:::

## Why it matters at the table

Almost every roll starts with an Attribute. Your highest Attribute is what your hunter reaches for first; your lowest is where they are vulnerable.

## Decide

- Is your hunter strongest in the **Physical**, **Social**, or **Mental** column?
- Which single Attribute describes them best? That one gets 4.
- What are they bad at? That one gets 1 — a weakness makes a character interesting.

| Dots | Count |
| ---- | ----- |
| 4    | 1     |
| 3    | 3     |
| 2    | 4     |
| 1    | 1     |

A short guide to each Attribute is in [Attributes and Skills](../../rules/attributes-skills.mdx).

## On your sheet

Every Attribute starts at 1. Click a dot to set its value.

:::note[Example: Lena]
Years of emergencies made Lena hard to rattle: **Composure 4**. She is quick and hardy — **Dexterity 3, Stamina 3, Wits 3**. **Strength, Charisma, Intelligence,** and **Resolve** sit at 2. She is a terrible liar: **Manipulation 1**.
:::

## Common questions

<details>
<summary>Why not put everything into one column?</summary>

You can lean on one column, but the spread forces some balance. Stamina also sets Health and Composure plus Resolve set Willpower, so very low values there make your hunter fragile.

</details>
