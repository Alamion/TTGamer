# Storyteller Characters \{#storyteller-characters}

A **Storyteller character** is someone played by the Storyteller, the person who portrays the world and the people the players meet. This simplified sheet records the few pools, tracks, and abilities needed to run that character in a scene. Use a full character sheet when detailed Attributes, Skills, or a hunter’s or vampire’s resources matter.

:::tip[In short]
Most people in a story never need Attributes and Skills. A **Storyteller character** has three dice pools, Health and Willpower, a few things it does better than its pools say, and notes. Vampires and hunters who stand against the players keep their full sheets.
:::

## What the sheet holds \{#what-the-sheet-holds}

- [Standard dice pools](#standard-pools): complete Physical, Social, and Mental pools.
- [Exceptional dice pools](#exceptional-pools): complete pools for named exceptions.
- **Health and Willpower**: four boxes each to start; the track's own **−** and **+** set the number of boxes, from one to fifteen. Damage marks work as on a player's sheet.
- **Disciplines and powers**: for ghouls, creatures, and anything with gifts. Names suggest the Disciplines and the hunters' Edges.
- **Type**: mortal, ghoul, animal, supernatural, or other — a note for you, with no rules attached.

## Type \{#types}

**Type** is a descriptive classification: Mortal, Ghoul, Animal, Supernatural, or Other. A **ghoul** is a living person sustained and changed by vampire blood; **Supernatural** covers creatures with unnatural abilities that do not fit the other categories. Changing Type labels the character but grants no powers, dice, or special rules automatically.

## Concept \{#concept}

**Concept** is a short description of who the character is and what makes them distinct, such as “homicide detective who notices impossible details.” It gives the Storyteller something to portray; Type classifies the creature, while Concept describes the individual.

## Standard dice pools \{#standard-pools}

**Standard dice pools** are three complete pools for broad kinds of action: **Physical** for bodily activity, **Social** for dealing with people, and **Mental** for thinking and noticing. Choose the one that fits and roll that many dice; do not add an Attribute and Skill again. A clerk might have Physical 2, Social 3, and Mental 3, while a soldier has 5, 2, and 3.

## Exceptional dice pools \{#exceptional-pools}

An **exceptional dice pool** is a complete pool for a named activity where the character differs from their broad standard pool. Use its number instead of the standard pool when that activity applies: a marksman with Physical 3 and Firearms 6 rolls six dice to shoot. It is neither a Skill rating to add nor a bonus; names such as “Reading lips” work as well as Skill names.

## Disciplines and powers \{#powers}

**Powers** on this sheet are the supernatural abilities available to the character. A **Discipline** is a category of vampiric ability; an individual power is one specific use of it, while other creatures may have unrelated gifts. Record the ability’s name and its rating in dots where applicable, and put activation, cost, and effect in its details or notes using the relevant ability’s rules. Listing an ability does not automatically apply its effects.

## Simple foes \{#simple-foes}

A **simple foe** is a minor opponent represented mainly by one **Difficulty**: the number of successes a player needs to overcome them. Difficulty 3, for example, requires three successes; falling short lets the foe hurt or obstruct the character as the scene demands. If the foe needs to roll, use a complete pool of twice that Difficulty. Set the Difficulty on the sheet and use **Roll 2 × Difficulty** when needed; usually the players roll against the target instead.

## Vampires and hunters as antagonists

A vampire or a hunter who opposes the players needs their Attributes, Skills, and powers, so make them on the ordinary vampire or hunter sheet and keep the **Brief** view open during play. To run them lightly, switch off **Track Hunger and Willpower** (for a hunter, **Track Willpower**): those trackers disappear, a vampire's rolls lose their Hunger dice, and the stored values come back when you switch it on again. Sheriff Ottilie Brandt is such a vampire.
