# Dice Pools

:::tip[In short]
Add an **Attribute** and a **Skill**, roll that many ten-sided dice, and count every die showing **6 or more**. Meet the **Difficulty** to succeed. Each **pair of 10s** is worth four successes.
:::

## When you roll

You only roll when the outcome is uncertain and it matters. Opening an unlocked door is not a roll; picking its lock while something scratches at the other side is.

## Building the pool

1. The Storyteller describes the obstacle and names what fits: usually one **Attribute** (what you are) and one **Skill** (what you know).
2. Add their dots together. That number is your **dice pool**.
3. Bonuses and penalties add or remove dice. A pool never drops below one die.

If you have no dots in the Skill, you still roll the Attribute alone — being untrained is hard, not impossible.

## Reading the dice

- Every die showing **6–9** is one success.
- Every **10** is one success too, but two 10s together form a **critical**: that pair counts as **four** successes instead of two.
- The Storyteller sets a **Difficulty** — the number of successes you need. Two is an ordinary challenge; four or more is hard.
- Successes beyond the Difficulty are your **margin**. A big margin means a cleaner, faster, or more impressive result.

:::note[Example: Lena]
Lena drives an ambulance through a flooded underpass, chased by something that should not swim. **Dexterity 3 + Driving 3** gives her six dice; the Difficulty is 3.

:::info[The site counts it for you]
Clicking a trait on a V5 sheet builds the pool for you — successes on 6+, no subtracted ones, no exploding dice. When the roll comes from a V5 sheet, or from the dice panel's **WoD** tab switched to **V5**, the roller also:

- counts each **pair of 10s** as four successes (the notation gains `x2=10`);
- rolls line dice — a hunter's **Desperation** or a vampire's **Hunger** — as **special dice**, added with the tab's special-die button (in notation, `:h` right after the dice, as in `3d10>=6 + 2d10:h>=6`), drawn in their own colour and listed separately in the roll history;
- names what the special dice mean: a Desperation 1, a messy critical, or a bestial failure. Give the Difficulty in the tab to get a definite answer; without it, outcomes that depend on success are phrased as “if the roll meets the Difficulty”.

With a Difficulty set in the tab, every roll made there also says whether it succeeded and by what margin, or how many successes it fell short.

Both behaviours are switches in the V5 mode of the WoD tab. A specialty is not added automatically: add its die yourself when it fits the action.
:::

## Spending Willpower to reroll

Once per roll you may spend a point of Willpower (mark one **superficial** box on your Willpower track) to reroll **up to three** dice that did not succeed. Keep the new results, good or bad.

## Tests of two sides

When two characters work against each other — a chase, an argument, a struggle over a weapon — both roll, and the side with more successes wins. The difference between the two totals is the winner's margin.
