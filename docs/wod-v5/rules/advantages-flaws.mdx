# Advantages and Flaws

:::tip[In short]
Advantages record benefits beyond Attributes and Skills. Flaws record lasting complications. Use the traits and ratings agreed for your game, rather than borrowing another line’s catalog or costs automatically.
:::

## Advantages \{#advantages}

**Advantages** are rated benefits that describe a character’s personal qualities, relationships, or supporting circumstances. They differ from Attributes (raw capacities), Skills (learned abilities), and carried equipment (individual possessions). A contact who provides information or access to a safe place can be an Advantage; a coat in your bag is equipment.

Record the trait’s **name**, its **rating** in dots, and **details** that make it usable in the story: who the contact is, what they know, or where the safe place lies. Dots express the scope specified by that trait’s rules; they are not a universal bonus to every roll. The Storyteller and the rules for your game determine which entries are available and what they cost.

## Flaws \{#flaws}

**Flaws** are rated, ongoing drawbacks that complicate a character’s life, such as an enemy or a persistent vulnerability. They are separate from damage currently marked on a track and from simply having a low Attribute or Skill.

Record the Flaw’s **name**, its **rating**, and **details** of when it matters. “Enemy” needs someone behind it: who they are, what they want, and how they can interfere. Use the rating and effects from the applicable rules, agreed with the Storyteller; recording dots does not automatically apply a penalty or award experience.
