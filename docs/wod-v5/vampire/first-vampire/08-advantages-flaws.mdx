# Advantages and Flaws

_Step 8 of 10_

:::tip[In short]
Spend **7 points** on Advantages (a haven, money, contacts, mortals who feed you) and take **2 points** of Flaws, on top of the ones your predator type gave.
:::

## Why it matters at the table

Advantages are the people and resources your vampire can call on outside a dice roll — and a vampire needs many: a safe place to sleep through the day, mortals who do not ask questions, money for both. Flaws give the Storyteller hooks: an enemy, a bad habit, a weakness of the Blood.

## Decide

- Where does your vampire sleep? A **Haven** of 1 dot is a hiding place; more dots make it safer and larger.
- Who would they call at 3 a.m.? That is a **Contact** or an **Ally**. Mortals they feed on regularly are a **Herd**.
- What from their mortal life did they keep: **Resources**, **Fame**, **Influence**?
- What makes their nights harder? Prey they refuse to touch (**Prey Exclusion**), habits from another age, an addiction, an enemy.

Your Storyteller has the full list of Merits, Backgrounds, and Flaws in the rulebook. Anything else, agree on together: 1 dot is small, 5 is huge.

## On your sheet

Add entries under **Advantages** and **Flaws** and set their dots. Write the predator type's ones with a note, so they are not counted twice.

:::note[Example: Mirela]

- Advantages: **Haven — studio above the gallery** (2), **Resources — restoration commissions** (2), **Contacts — art dealers and auction houses** (2), **Herd — regulars of the late openings** (1). Total 7, plus **Looks: Beautiful** (2) from the Siren.
- Flaws: **Prey Exclusion — the gallery staff** (1), **Living in the Past** (1). Total 2, plus the Siren's **Enemy** (1).
  :::

:::info[Checkpoint]

- Advantages add up to 7 and Flaws to 2, without the predator type's.
  :::
