# Condition

Condition collects the values that describe injury, mental reserves, and responses to pressure. The full sheet places these together; the brief separates Health from its Resolve block.

## Health \{#health}

Health is the injury track that records how badly a character is hurt. The classic second-edition sheet has seven levels: Bruised (0), Hurt (−1), Injured (−1), Wounded (−2), Mauled (−2), Crippled (−5), and Incapacitated. A slash marks ordinary damage and a cross marks aggravated damage. The deepest marked level supplies the displayed penalty; Incapacitated means the character cannot take normal actions, even though its row has no numerical penalty.

Use the appropriate wound penalty when resolving actions under the group's rules. Resolve the attack, damage, and any resistance first, then record the resulting injuries: marking a box does not roll damage, perform a resistance check, or heal the character. These classic rules are separate from the Star Wars conversion's bashing/lethal distinction and steeper penalties. See the [tracker guide](../../guide/template-editor/elements.mdx#trackers) for marking controls.

Characters saved before this correction used the conversion's two damage types. On opening one, its old bashing and lethal wounds become ordinary wounds at the same positions, and a notice explains the change. The original seven marks remain in `legacyHealth` in the character's JSON export for recovery. New aggravated wounds are recorded explicitly; re-opening or importing an updated character does not convert them again.

## Willpower \{#willpower}

Willpower is mental resolve, represented by a lasting rating and a reserve of available points. The rating is the maximum; current points show what remains to spend, never more than that maximum. For example, 2 / 5 means two points available out of a rating of five. Spending reduces current points, while recovery restores them without raising the rating.

Where the chosen game's rules allow it, spend a point for exceptional effort, such as one automatic success or resistance to an impulse; the classic automatic-success option is limited to one point per turn and can have character-type restrictions. Agree eligibility with the Storyteller. Recovery can follow meaningful actions that fulfil [Nature](../character/index.mdx#nature), restored confidence, or the end of a story as the Storyteller decides. There is no automatic recovery timer on the sheet. Record changes with the [pool control](../../guide/template-editor/elements.mdx#built-in-parts). Some settings replace these uses; they are not rules for the Star Wars conversion's static Willpower.

## Initiative \{#initiative}

Initiative determines the order in which characters act when timing matters. The displayed standard value is **Wits + Alertness**: the number of dice in the classic initiative pool, rather than a completed initiative result. “Std” means standard, distinguishing this calculation from a setting's alternative. For example, Wits 3 and Alertness 2 give five dice.

In the classic procedure, roll that pool at difficulty 4 and compare successes; more successes act earlier. The Storyteller can adjust the difficulty for the situation. The sheet calculates the pool, but the player still makes the roll and handles any agreed adjustments; it does not arrange a combat turn automatically.

## Virtues \{#virtues}

Virtues are rated traits describing a character's moral and emotional responses: **Conscience** concerns right and wrong, **Self-Control** concerns restraint, and **Courage** concerns fear. Each uses one to five dots on this sheet. They give the Storyteller a basis for checks under pressure, beyond the free-text descriptions of personality. The chosen game or setting determines the specific consequences of those checks; vampire frenzy, Humanity, and supernatural resources are not universal features of this generic sheet.

## Conscience \{#conscience}

Conscience measures a character's capacity to recognise wrongdoing and feel remorse. It matters when a rule calls for a moral response, such as assessing the aftermath of harming an innocent person. Unlike Nature or a written moral code, it has a numerical rating that can supply dice for a check. The Storyteller decides when such a check applies under the chosen game; the generic sheet does not calculate moral decline from this rating.

## Self-Control \{#self-control}

Self-Control measures the ability to restrain impulses and stay composed under pressure. A character insulted during a tense negotiation might struggle to keep from striking back. Use the rating if the agreed rules call for a restraint check; otherwise describe the choice through roleplay. The rating alone does not introduce vampire frenzy or any rule tied to feeding.

## Courage \{#courage}

Courage measures the ability to face fear and keep acting despite it. A character crossing an exposed walkway to reach a trapped friend might need a Courage check if the Storyteller calls for one. In the classic creation procedure, starting Willpower equals Courage; the generic sheet does not initialise or recalculate Willpower from this trait. Set the starting resource as agreed, then track and advance Willpower separately.
