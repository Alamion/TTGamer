# Advantages

## Advantages \{#advantages}

Advantages is the sheet section for additional resources, benefits, and complications beyond Attributes and Abilities. Backgrounds describe rated resources and relationships; Merits give particular benefits; Flaws introduce difficulties. Flaws appear here because the section collects these extra character options together, even though their effects are disadvantageous.

## Backgrounds \{#backgrounds}

Backgrounds are rated resources, relationships, or circumstances a character can draw on. They are different from Biography, which is the written story of the character's past. A reliable contact at a newspaper or access to a workshop could be a Background if the group's rules support it. Each entry records a name and dot rating; agree what that rating provides with the Storyteller rather than treating every entry as an automatic dice bonus.

## Merits \{#merits}

Merits are special beneficial traits that give a character a particular edge outside the main ratings. Record the chosen Merit's name and point value, with enough description to remember its effect. Available choices, costs, and purchase rules depend on the agreed rules and setting; entering a point value does not itself pay for the Merit or apply its effect to a roll.

## Flaws \{#flaws}

Flaws are traits that give a character disadvantages or recurring complications. Record each Flaw's name and point value, and agree how it affects play: a persistent enemy, for example, creates trouble through the story. Whether a Flaw grants creation points, and how many may be taken, depends on the group's chosen rules. The generic sheet sets no universal allowance and does not automatically exchange these values for other traits.
