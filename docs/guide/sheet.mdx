# Character sheet \{#character-sheet}

:::tip[In short]
**New** on the [character sheet](/universal_sheet) makes a document for a game system; type into it and every change is saved on this device at once. **Export** saves a document to a file, **Import** brings files back, also on another device. The [documents window](./documents) keeps your documents in folders and lays several of them out side by side.
:::

## The toolbar \{#toolbar}

| Button        | What it does                                                                                 |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Export**    | saves the current document to a file                                                         |
| **Import**    | reads one or more files back                                                                 |
| **Library**   | opens the [library](./template-editor/library) of rules, settings, document types, and pages |
| **Manage**    | opens the [documents window](./documents)                                                    |
| **Reset**     | puts the current document back to its defaults                                               |
| **New**       | makes a new document                                                                         |
| **View mode** | chooses how the document is shown ([views](#views))                                          |

The _current_ document is the one you last clicked or typed in; **Export** and **Reset** act on it. With no document open, the page invites you to create one, and the tools you open float above it.

## Creating a document \{#new}

**New** lists every kind of document the way the library groups them: by rules, then by setting, for example a Star Wars character, droid, creature, vehicle, or fodder group under World of Darkness 2nd Edition, and the Hunter and Vampire characters under the 5th Edition, where the rules' own kinds — the mortal, the [Storyteller character, and the group](/docs/wod-v5/storyteller) — sit in their own folded group. Your own [document types](./template-editor/types-and-settings#document-types) and settings appear under the rules they are built on, with their descriptions.

- **Search.** The cursor starts in the search field: type part of a kind's, setting's, or rules' name, in either language (`vamp` or `вамп`). When one kind is left it is chosen; the arrow keys move through the list, `Enter` goes on to the name, and `Escape` clears the search (a second `Escape` closes the dialog).
- **Recent.** The last five kinds you created, newest first, come at the top, each with its rules and setting beside it. The rules start open and the settings inside them folded: open or fold any of them by its heading, and the dialog remembers it on this device.
- **Start from.** A kind that ships an example (Jax Vorn, Lena Varga, Mirela Voss, the wampa, the stormtrooper squad, the vehicles) can start as **Blank** or as a copy of the example. The copy is yours: changing it changes no other copy.
- **Name and folder.** Type a name, or leave it empty to type it later on the sheet; the name shows on the tab and in the sheet's Name field. **Folder** starts at the top level (or at the folder you added the document from) and opens a folder list to pick another.

`Enter` in the name, or **Create**, makes the document; it opens on the page the library chose for its type. On a phone the dialog fills the screen: choose the kind first, then the details, with **Back to kinds** to return.

## Editing and saving \{#saving}

Type, click ratings, add list entries: there is no save button. Every change is written on this device right away, so a reload keeps it. There is no undo for values you type; what a button changed can be undone from its message.

- **Portraits and images.** **Upload image** takes a picture from the device (up to 10 MB; it is shrunk and kept on this device only), or paste an `https://` address of a picture on the web. The **×** removes it. A character's portrait card shows once **Show portrait** is on: in **Appearance description** or the biography, on a Storyteller character in its identity card. A document that already had a portrait keeps showing it.
- **Rolls.** A trait with a dice icon rolls its pool: a left click puts the pool into the dice roller, a right click rolls at once. See [rolling from a sheet](./dice-roller#sheet-rolls).
- **Buttons.** Some pages have buttons that roll and change values — on a vampire sheet the **Rouse check** (Hunger rises on a failure), a Rouse check on each power with a cost, and the **Remorse check** (with stains: a failure costs a dot of Humanity, and the stains clear). The message names what changed and offers **Undo** for a few seconds; a value you changed in the meantime is kept. A button that does not apply now is greyed out and says why. Template authors make their own: see [buttons](./template-editor/elements#buttons).
- **Several documents.** Each open document keeps its own values; see [several documents side by side](./documents#layout).
- **Links between documents.** A linked document (a group's member, a character's group) opens with its open icon. The browser's **Back** returns to the document you came from and **Forward** goes on again; the address names the shown document, so a reload keeps it.

## Resources \{#resources}

A resource control records two numbers: the current points and the maximum capacity, such as 2 points out of a maximum of 5. It can show those numbers as dots, as editable numbers, or as filled and outlined boxes. The game's rules say what the points represent and whether they can be spent.

- **Dots.** Use the rows labelled **Current** and **Maximum**. Click or tap the dot for the value you want; pressing the last filled dot lowers that value by one. With a keyboard, focus a dot with `Tab` and press `Enter` or `Space`.
- **Numbers.** In `2 / 5`, the first number is current and the second is maximum. Select either number to type it, or use the arrow keys. On a brief, these number inputs may replace the two dot rows.
- **Boxes.** Filled boxes count current points; outlined boxes show the maximum. Click or tap to fill up to a box, or press the last filled box to lower current by one. Right click to set the maximum; on a focused box use `Shift+Enter` or `Shift+Space`, or long press on touch. Pressing the last outlined box with that action lowers the maximum by one. A plain `Enter` or `Space` changes current instead. If a legend is shown, select its current or maximum mark, then click boxes; press the mark again or `Escape` to stop.

Values stay within the field's limits. Lowering maximum also lowers current if needed; current usually stops at maximum, though a game may let raising current raise maximum too. Darker dots or boxes mark a minimum that cannot be cleared. A maximum derived from other values may impose another limit.

This pair differs from a [rating](./template-editor/elements.mdx#rating), which has one numerical value, and a [damage tracker](./template-editor/elements.mdx#trackers), whose boxes record kinds of damage or other marks. Filled boxes here count points, rather than cycling through damage types.

## Resource tracking switch \{#resource-tracking}

The resource-tracking switch chooses whether a Hunter or Vampire sheet shows certain changing resources during play. **Track Willpower** on a Hunter controls the Willpower tracker. **Track Hunger and Willpower** on a Vampire controls the Hunger and Willpower controls and the main **Rouse check** button.

Click or tap the switch to turn it on or off; with a keyboard, focus it and press `Enter` or `Space`. Turning it off hides those controls without clearing their saved values. Turn it on again to show the values you had before.

While Vampire tracking is off, rolls from that document do not automatically replace ordinary dice with Hunger dice. Turning tracking on resumes that replacement using the saved Hunger value. The switch leaves other controls, such as Health, visible.

## Equipment \{#equipment}

The equipment editor is a list of weapons, armor, and other items, with editable details for each item. Weapons, armor, inventory, and implants offer different fields, according to the page and game.

Use **Add Weapon**, **Add Armor**, **Add Item**, or the list's other add button to create an entry. Expand an item's heading to see its details. Type its name; where catalog suggestions appear, select one to fill the available statistics. You can also enter your own item. The copied statistics remain editable, so check them before play and adjust them for your item. Use the item's **×** (**Remove item**) to remove its entry.

Edit the fields that are present: for example damage and range on a weapon, armor statistics on armor, or description, effects, weight, price, and quantity in inventory. **Equipped**, where shown, records whether that item is in use; switch it on or off rather than deleting an item you still carry.

Weapons with **Ammo** and **Capacity** keep current ammunition and its maximum. Edit **Ammo** after firing or reloading; typing damage or making an ordinary sheet roll does not spend ammunition for you. Inventory may similarly show **Quantity** and **Max qty**. These controls record your choices; the game's equipment rules determine damage, protection, costs, and when an item can be used.

## System slots \{#system-slots}

**System slots** is an editable table of named vehicle systems and their **Damaged** switches. Each row records one system you want to track, rather than a fixed physical compartment in the vehicle.

Use the table's add button to make a row, type its name under **System**, and switch **Damaged** on or off as its condition changes. The table holds up to ten rows; a row's remove button deletes that row. A switch can also be focused with `Tab` and changed with `Enter` or `Space`.

These switches are separate from the vehicle's overall damage track and from the [Configuration](#vehicle-configuration) list: editing one does not update the others. Record the outcome agreed at the table here; see the Star Wars [specific-damage rules](../star-wars-wod-2e/vehicles-mechanisms/durability-damage-repair.mdx#specific-damage) for when a particular system is affected.

## Vehicle configuration \{#vehicle-configuration}

**Configuration** is a list of labels naming the systems of a vehicle, for example engines, sensors, or a hyperdrive. It provides a named list to refer to during play, including when the Star Wars ion-damage rules call for a system to be chosen.

Expand **Configuration**, use its add button to create a row, and type the system's name in **Label**. Edit a label directly to rename it, or use the row's remove button to delete it. The list holds up to twenty entries.

Configuration stores names; [System slots](#system-slots) also stores a **Damaged** switch for each of its own rows. Maintain these records separately: a label does not create a slot or change a damage mark. The sheet does not choose a disabled system or time its recovery for you; follow the [ion-damage rules](../star-wars-wod-2e/vehicles-mechanisms/durability-damage-repair.mdx#ion-damage) and record the result.

## Views and briefs \{#views}

**View mode** lists the ways a document can be shown: its full sheet, its **Brief**, and pages you made for it in the [template editor](./template-editor). The choice is saved with the document. When two or more documents are open, each group has its own selector instead, which changes only that group.

A **brief** is the short view a GM glances at during play: ratings as short labels with their values, laid out by the width of the place it is drawn in, about one phone screen high. Hover, focus, or tap a short label to read the full name. A brief is still editable, and a document opened beside another starts in it.

## Export and import \{#export-import}

**Export** downloads the current document as a `ttgamer_<name>.json` file. It holds the document and its view; a document of your own type carries the type inside, so it opens where the type is not installed. Pictures uploaded from the device stay behind (pictures by address travel), and so do its folder and your pages: pages go through the library's [export](./template-editor/library#library-export).

**Import** takes one file or many at once; a broken file never stops the others, and each document goes to the end of the top level and becomes current.

- **Already there.** A document that is already on this device asks: **Replace** it, import a **Duplicate** with a new identity, or **Cancel** this file. A different version of one of your document types asks the same about the type. With several files, **Apply this choice to the other conflicts in this import** answers once for all.
- **The summary.** When something was repaired or not imported, a summary lists every file with its reason. Files that could not be imported are kept in the documents window, so they are not lost: see [rejected files](./documents#rejected-files).
- **Old exports.** Characters exported by the first versions of the site still import as Star Wars characters, until 13 April 2027.

## Reset \{#reset}

**Reset** asks first, then puts the current document's values back to their defaults, empties the fields of your own pages, and clears its name; an uploaded portrait is deleted. The document keeps its place, its type, and its page. It cannot be undone, so export first if in doubt.

## Game terms \{#game-terms}

On the Russian site, **Game terms** chooses how rules terms are named on sheets: Russian names with a hint showing the English rulebook name (the default), the English book names, or Russian names without hints. A tip under the toolbar explains the hints until you dismiss it. The choice is kept in this browser.

## Publisher badge \{#badge}

A document built on material a publisher shares under a community policy (such as the World of Darkness 5th Edition material under the [Dark Pack](../wod-v5/dark-pack)) shows the policy's badge, which links to its page. It is shown once on each surface: under the page, at the end of the toolbar when several groups fill the screen, or at the foot of a floating window. Exports of such documents carry the notice text.

## Where your data is kept \{#storage}

Documents, folders, your pages and types, uploaded pictures, the layout, and your settings stay in this browser on this device: nothing is sent to a server, and there is no account. Another device or browser does not see them. To move them, export the documents and import the files there (pictures uploaded from the device do not travel). Clearing the site's data in the browser, or closing a private window, deletes everything; exported files are your backup.
